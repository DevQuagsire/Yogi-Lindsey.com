About

Presence in healing spaces

Lindsey is a trauma-informed wellness practitioner who brings yoga, breathwork, mindfulness, and sound healing into environments where people are rebuilding their lives.

You don’t have to have everything figured out to begin healing.

Through trauma-informed yoga, mindfulness, breathwork, and sound healing, I create safe, accessible practices that help people reconnect with their bodies, regulate their nervous systems, and begin finding their way back to themselves.

Core Approach

More than movement

Yoga doesn’t have to be about flexibility, difficult poses, or shaping a “perfect” body. Lindsey’s approach is about awareness, choice, and coming home to yourself—at a pace that feels safe.

Mindfulness

Learning to notice thoughts, emotions, sensations, and the present moment without judgment.

Breathwork

Exploring breathing techniques that support awareness, grounding, and a clearer connection between mind and body.

Slow, accessible movement

Gentle, intentional movement that helps people listen to their bodies and rebuild trust in their physical experience.

Sound healing

Using sound and vibration to support relaxation, reflection, and space for stillness.

Trauma-informed practices

Creating an environment with real choices and respected boundaries— where nothing is forced on the body or nervous system before it’s ready.

Who I Serve

Meeting people where they are

Lindsey has experience supporting people navigating many kinds of healing and transition—always with care, pacing, and respect for each person’s story.

  • Substance use treatment and recovery
  • Detox and residential treatment settings
  • Behavioral health programs
  • Halfway houses and transitional living
  • Correctional and re-entry environments
  • Neurodivergent communities, including autism and developmental differences
  • Mental health challenges, stress, anxiety, and emotional overwhelm
  • Those who feel disconnected from their bodies
  • People rebuilding after difficult experiences

No previous yoga experience is needed. Classes are adaptable, accessible, and centered on safety—not performance.
